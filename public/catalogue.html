The opportunity

The QR code solved access. It did not solve the menu.

Hospitality operators need more than a link to a PDF. They need a living sales surface that feels local, stays current, and is simple enough to manage during a busy service.

“
The menu is where operations, brand, and buying decisions meet.

mofé starts with this narrow, high-frequency surface and builds a trusted operating layer around it.

01

Generic tools flatten local identity.

LTR assumptions, poor Persian typography, and template-heavy design make many digital menus feel imported rather than native to the venue and its guests.

02

Static files become stale.

Price changes, sold-out items, and seasonal additions require a management workflow—not repeated design exports, file replacements, and reprinted codes.

03

Complex software loses the operator.

Independent cafés do not need enterprise restaurant software to maintain a menu. They need the essential controls, in their language, with almost no setup burden.

04

The guest experience is the final test.

A menu must open quickly on ordinary phones and weak connections, remain legible one-handed, and ask nothing from the guest before showing what is available.

Product foundation

Lightweight at the table. Serious underneath.

The current implementation already covers the complete data path—from authenticated venue management to a secure, cached public menu.

01
Operator workspacePersian-first menu and venue management
02
Structured catalogueCategories, items, variants, prices, allergens, media
03
Static menu rendererEscaped content, sanitized color, self-hosted typography

Guest deliveryCached public HTML through one venue URL
A

Localized by architecture, not translation

RTL layout, Persian formatting, bilingual item structure, local currency, and native type choices are present at the data and rendering layers.

B

Operational detail already modeled

Variants, multiple price descriptions, calories, 14 allergen codes, category states, sold-out flags, ordering, and soft deletion reflect real menu operations.

C

Performance is part of the product

Photos are converted to compact WebP assets, public output is server-rendered HTML, fonts are self-hosted, and menu responses are cached.

D

A security-conscious baseline

Hashed sessions, strict cookies, CSRF validation, authorization checks, rate limiting, escaped public content, and formula-safe CSV handling are implemented.

E

Simple deployment supports iteration

A typed Next.js application, Prisma data layer, SQLite deployment path, and automated test suite keep the MVP compact and understandable.

Commercialization

A narrow entry point with room to become a hospitality layer.

The initial product solves one visible, recurring job. The commercial path can begin with simple venue subscriptions and expand only where operator behavior earns it.

Forward-looking model hypothesis — not current revenue or traction
